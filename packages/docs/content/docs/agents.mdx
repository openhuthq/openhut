<Steps>
<Step>

### Make it

**New agent** in the sidebar. Pick a face and a name, like Support, Sales or Ops. Choose its **Model**, where it **Runs on**, and **Access**: the people who can use it. Then **Create agent**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Tell it its job

Just say hello. In its first chat a new agent asks you a few questions about the job, then writes its own instructions. You can also open **Setup** (the gear, or click the agent's name at the top of the chat) and write **What it does** yourself, the way you'd brief a new teammate. The agent reads it before every reply.

Threads and the Setup gear sit at the top right of the chat; Clear chat sits next to attach in the message box. Click the agent's face or name at the top of the chat to open Setup, where you change what it does, its model, apps, channels, memory, skills, routines and webhooks. **Threads** opens the agent's threads in a card on the right. While the agent uses its browser, a small **Browser** window shows the page live; close it and the globe at the top of the chat brings it back.

Every agent starts out talking the same way: the answer first, short, in plain words, with no preamble or closing offers, and with lists, tables and code blocks only when they help. In Slack, Telegram and WhatsApp it uses that app's own formatting. Your instructions win, so write "explain in detail" or "use headings" there if you want something else. In the app, its replies show tables, task lists and code with highlighting and a **Copy** button.

</Step>
<Step>

### Give it a channel and apps

In **Setup › Channels**, connect [Slack](/slack), [Telegram](/telegram), [WhatsApp](/whatsapp) or [Discord](/discord). In **Setup › Connections**, switch on the [apps](/apps) it may use, or **Add app** to connect a new one. Or just chat with it in OpenHut.

</Step>
</Steps>

The button next to its name starts and stops it. **Delete** removes it with its chats, memory and skills. Double-click the name to rename it. Click its face at the top of the chat to change its name and picture: pick a face, **Shuffle**, or **Upload picture**. A name can use letters, digits, spaces, dots, dashes and underscores. A connected channel in **Setup › Channels** opens its details when you click it.

## What it did

Above each reply, one quiet line says what the agent did for it, like *Queried Postgres twice and searched the web*, with the time it took when that was over 10 seconds. Click it to see each step; click a step to see what went in and what came back. While it works, the steps it finished stay folded in that line and a second line under it says what it's doing now, from the moment a step starts. **Stop** stops the agent's work too, so a message you send next isn't stuck behind it; a reply stopped before it said anything shows *Stopped.* A message that has to wait for an earlier task says *Waiting for an earlier task to finish* until the agent starts on it.

A step that failed says why, in red, and the line counts it (*1 failed*). A step that asks you for something, like connecting an app, shows as its own card under the reply instead of in the line. A turn that only used tools, with nothing to say, shows just that line. If the whole turn failed, the line stays open, the reply gives the reason, and **Retry** asks again. A turn that ends without an answer, is interrupted, or loses its connection to the agent says so in a *Stopped:* line, so no message goes unanswered. Something blocked by a [permission](/permissions) shows a card with the fix.

## Access

**Access** is who can see and chat with an agent. Pick **Specific people** from your [team](/people); you, as its owner, always have access. Turn on **Whole team** to give everyone access. Admins can always open every agent. Only its owner or an admin changes Access.

## Posting on its own

An agent can post in its own channels without anyone messaging it first, like a morning report in your team's Slack, Discord or Telegram. It can post only where it already answers: the Slack or Discord channels you picked for it, and the Telegram or WhatsApp chats of people allowed to message it. Ask it in a chat, or in its instructions, to "post the numbers in #ops every morning".

**Notify** sets where it tells people when nobody is talking to it, like after a webhook call or a routine: a default Discord channel and, in plain words, rules such as "outages go to #alerts, everything else to #ops". Each message it posts this way shows under its reply in the app.

## Run code and Browser

In **Setup › General**, **Run code** lets the agent run code in its own container. **Browser** gives it a real web browser. While it browses, a small **Browser** window shows the page live; **Open full size** lets you take over to log in. A web page can carry text written to mislead an agent, so turn Browser on only for agents that need it.

## Sub-agents

For a big job, an agent can hand parts to sub-agents that work at the same time, then answer with what they found. The chat waits until they finish. Each sub-agent costs model tokens like a chat of its own.

While they work, the agent shows as working in the sidebar.

## Changes it asks for

An agent changes its own name and instructions by itself, when you or the person it works for ask it to in a chat. The change applies at once, with no approval, and shows in the chat as a card: *Changed its instructions*, with what changed (removed lines struck through, new lines marked +) and **Applied**. **Undo** puts the old name and instructions back; anyone who can edit the agent in Setup can use it, and the agent hears that you undid it. It's told never to change its instructions because of something it read in a web page, file or message, but read the card: nothing stops a change before it happens any more.

For everything else, an agent asks to change its own settings. The request shows in the chat with what it wants, one short line from the agent saying what it's for (every request has one, and it stays on the line once you've answered), the fields you can edit before you agree (a key to paste), **Approve** and **Not now**. The agent hears your answers once, together: when nothing it asked for in that chat is still waiting, or 30 seconds after your last answer. Most changes reach it on its next turn, in the same conversation, without a restart: a new name or instructions, apps you give or take away, keys, its model, and **Run code**.

A change you make in the app saves at once; the agent shows **Updating…** for the moment it takes to reach it. If it can't, it shows **Couldn’t apply your last change** with **Retry**.

Only a few changes restart it: adding or removing a channel, changing who can message it on Slack, Discord or WhatsApp, a new token, a model from another provider, **Browser**, and moving it to another computer. Those wait until it has finished what it was doing, and it hears about them after the restart. A message you send while it starts or restarts waits and goes through once it's ready.
